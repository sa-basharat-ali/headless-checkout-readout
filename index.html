A headless checkout lifted conversion 6.1 points. The launch dashboard said 13.2.

A synthetic fiat-to-crypto on-ramp, March to September 2026. Four wallet partners turn on an embedded Apple Pay checkout on May 14 (no redirect, no re-authentication), ten more on July 1, ten on August 15, sixteen never. Every headless session is also played out under the old widget with the same random draws, so the true effect is known: 4,578 purchases and $1.04M of volume caused by the launch.

The launch landed in a market rally: partners that hadn't launched yet gained 6.3 points over the same four weeks. Headless sessions are also all iOS and more often returning verified users (58% vs 51%), so a session-level comparison credits the checkout with who the users already were. Split by user type, diff-in-diff lands within about a point of the truth on every wave, and returning users gain about four times what new users do.

Chargebacks arrive weeks to months after the purchase (median 45 days here), and most headless volume is recent. Headless is safer, because Apple Pay is, but the early read overstates the gap and understates headless's own rate about sixfold.

What surprised me

I expected re-weighting by user type to fix the pooled diff-in-diff. It barely moved it (7.0 to 6.7). Per wave and per user type the method was already right; the leftover error came from pooling returning and new users, who respond differently to the rally, across windows where their shares were shifting. The fix is to report the effect by user type, not to correct the total harder.
